# Getting Started with Celune

Welcome to Celune -- your AI-powered development workspace. This guide walks you through everything from creating your account to launching your first project with a team of AI agents.

---

## Sign Up

Celune is currently in early access. To create an account:

1. Go to [app.celune.ai/signup](https://app.celune.ai/signup).
2. Enter your **email** and create a **password**.
3. Enter an **access code**. Every new account requires one during the early access period.
4. If you do not have a code, click **Join the Waitlist** on the signup page. You will be notified by email when a spot opens up.
5. Once your code is accepted, your account is created and you move straight into onboarding.

> **Tip:** Access codes are sometimes shared at events, in communities, or by existing Celune users. Check your network if you are looking for one.

---

## Onboarding Chat

Onboarding is not a form -- it is a conversation. After signing up, you will meet **RICK**, your AI lead agent. RICK chats with you in a natural, streaming conversation to understand how you work and what you are building.

### What RICK will ask

- **What you do** -- your role, your domain, and the kind of work you focus on.
- **What you are building** -- the projects or products you are working on right now.
- **Workspace name** -- a name for your workspace (you can change this later).

### What happens next

Based on your answers, RICK creates a **personalized agent team** tailored to your needs. Each agent has its own specialty -- more on that in the "Meet Your Agents" section below.

### Personality customization

During onboarding, you can adjust RICK's **humor slider** -- this sets the tone between "all business" and "smart colleague at a coffee shop." It is the only personality control during onboarding, but you can fine-tune additional sliders (warmth, directness, formality, verbosity) later from each agent's settings page.

### Voice greeting

Your agent may play a short voice greeting so you can hear what they sound like. Voice features like text-to-speech and voice cloning are available after onboarding on supported plans.

## Your Workspace

Once onboarding is complete, you land on your **Dashboard**. This is your home base.

### Summary cards

The top of the dashboard shows at-a-glance metrics for your workspace:

- **Agents** -- how many AI agents are on your team.
- **Tasks** -- total active tasks across all projects.
- **Projects** -- the number of projects in your workspace.
- **Memory** -- entries stored in your agents' shared knowledge base.
- **Activity** -- recent actions and events.

### Getting-started checklist

Below the summary cards, you will see a **getting-started checklist** with suggested next steps. These guide you through connecting tools, creating tasks, and exploring key features. Items check off automatically as you complete them.

### Activity feed

The bottom of the dashboard shows a live **activity feed** -- a timeline of everything happening in your workspace, including task updates, agent actions, and project changes.

## Connect Your Tools

All integrations are optional. You can set them up during onboarding or later from **Settings**. Here is how to connect each one.

### GitHub

Connect GitHub to let your agents access repositories, understand your codebase, and work with pull requests.

1. Go to **Settings** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Connections**.
3. Click **Install GitHub App**.
4. GitHub will prompt you to select an account or organization.
5. Choose which **repositories** to grant access to -- you can select all or pick specific ones.
6. Confirm the installation. Celune will sync your selected repos.

> **Tip:** You can change which repositories Celune can access at any time from your GitHub App settings.

### AI Provider Keys (BYOK)

Celune uses a **Bring Your Own Key** model for AI operations. You provide an API key, and Celune uses it to power agent conversations and task execution in your workspace.

1. Go to **Settings** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Developer**.
3. Click **Add API Key**.
4. Paste your key -- Celune **auto-detects the provider** from the key prefix.

Supported providers:

| Provider      | Key prefix |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| Anthropic     | `sk-ant-`  |
| OpenAI        | `sk-`      |
| Google Gemini | `AI`       |
| xAI           | `xai-`     |

Your key is encrypted before storage and never displayed in the UI after saving.

> **Tip:** You need at least one API key to use agent features. If you are not sure which provider to start with, Anthropic (Claude) is the default recommendation.

### Slack

Connect Slack to receive notifications and interact with your agents from your existing Slack workspace.

1. Go to **Settings** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Connections**.
3. Click **Connect Slack**.
4. Authorize Celune in the Slack OAuth flow.
5. Choose which channels Celune can post to.

## Meet Your Agents

Your agent team is created during onboarding based on your conversation with RICK. Here is how the team works.

### Lead agent

**RICK** is your lead agent -- the one you talked to during onboarding. RICK coordinates work, manages projects, and is your primary point of contact. Think of RICK as your AI team lead.

### Supporting agents

Behind RICK, you have a team of **supporting agents**, each with a different specialty. Some focus on research, others on code review, analysis, or creative work. The exact team composition depends on what you told RICK during onboarding.

You can view your full agent roster on the **Agents** page. Each agent has its own chat interface, personality, and optional voice.

### Personality sliders

Every agent has five personality sliders you can adjust:

- **Humor** -- from straightforward to playful.
- **Warmth** -- from neutral to encouraging.
- **Directness** -- from diplomatic to blunt.
- **Formality** -- from casual to professional.
- **Verbosity** -- from concise to detailed.

Find these in each agent's settings by clicking the agent's name on the Agents page.

## Your First Task

Tasks are the core unit of work in Celune. Here is how to create one.

1. Go to the **Tasks** page in the sidebar.
2. Click **New Task**.
3. **Describe what you need** -- write a clear summary of the work. Be as specific as you like.
4. **Assign an agent** -- pick which agent should handle the task.
5. Click **Create**.

### Task lifecycle

Every task moves through a simple lifecycle:

- **Inbox** -- the task has been created but no one has started it yet.
- **In Progress** -- an agent (or you) is actively working on it.
- **Done** -- the work is complete.

There are additional statuses for more complex workflows (in review, blocked, archived), but inbox, in progress, and done are the three you will use most often.

> **Tip:** You can also create tasks by chatting with an agent. Just describe what you need in conversation, and the agent can turn it into a tracked task.

## Your First Project

Projects group related tasks together and give you a higher-level view of progress.

1. Go to the **Projects** page in the sidebar.
2. Click **New Project**.
3. Give it a name and description.
4. Choose a **project type**.

### Project types

Celune supports four project types, each with its own workflow:

- **Feature** -- building something new. Follows a lifecycle from planning through implementation to launch.
- **System** -- infrastructure, tooling, or internal improvements. Structured for technical work.
- **Research** -- exploration, investigation, or learning. Designed for open-ended work that produces findings rather than shipped code.
- **Plan** -- strategic planning, roadmaps, or coordination. Used when the output is a plan or decision rather than a deliverable.

Each project type has its own lifecycle with stages appropriate to that kind of work. You can add tasks to a project from the project page or by assigning a project when creating a task.
